## 2. Every judgement call, with justification

### 2.1 `(sps - 1)`, not `sps`, converts a slope to a rise

The brief says "multiplied by `(sps - 1)`" for `ols_slope` and I kept that,
but it is worth recording *why* it is not arbitrary, because it is the hinge
the whole estimator-interchangeability property hangs on. A segment spans
sample indices `0 .. sps-1`, so the fitted line's rise between its own first
and last sample is `slope * (sps - 1)`. Using `sps` would make `ols_slope`
disagree with `endpoints` on a noiseless ramp by a factor of `sps/(sps-1)` —
1 % at sps=100 — which is small enough to look like float noise and large
enough to break the exact-string tests. Test 12 (all four estimators
produce identical strings on noiseless signals) exists specifically to
prevent that from silently regressing.

### 2.2 `seg_slope` for the endpoint estimators

`details["seg_slope"]` is documented as "per-segment fitted slope (per
sample)", but `endpoints` and `robust_endpoints` do not fit anything. I set
it to the **secant slope** `delta / (sps - 1)` — the slope of the line
through the two points the estimator actually compared. That is the honest
per-sample reading of what those estimators measured, it makes
`seg_slope * (sps - 1) == delta` hold identically for all four estimators,
and it lets panel 1 of the figure draw one line per segment with one code
path regardless of estimator.

### 2.3 Training deltas are a **slice** of the data deltas

`psax()` recomputes its training PAA from a training slice that it
re-normalises with the training slice's *own* mean and std, and derives the
training segment count from `floor(dim_ratio * training_len)` — which can
imply a slightly different segment length from the `sps` actually used on
the data.

Both of those are harmless for a mean and **actively wrong for a delta**:

- a delta's magnitude scales linearly with segment length, so learning
  cutlines over segments of length `training_len // training_nseg` and
  applying them to segments of length `sps` biases every threshold;
- a separately-computed training standard deviation puts the cutlines in a
  different unit from the deltas being quantised.

So dSAX takes `training_deltas = deltas[:n_train_seg]` with
`n_train_seg = clip(training_len // sps, 2, data_nseg)`. Segment *i* of
`data[:n*sps]` **is** segment *i* of `data`, so this is exactly "the deltas
of the first `training_len` samples", in the identical unit, by
construction. It makes that entire class of bug unrepresentable. In the
normal case (`training_len == len(data)`, which is what the adapter always
passes) it is numerically identical to what psax would have done anyway.

The `clip(..., 2, ...)` lower bound means a pathologically short
`training_len` silently trains on two segments rather than raising. That is
the right trade: `training_len` is an advisory "learn from this much", not
a correctness-critical input, and every caller in this repo passes the full
length.

### 2.4 Degenerate delta distributions: **two** cases, not one

This was an actual bug I wrote and then caught with the smoke test, and it
is the single most important judgement call in the file.

A perfectly noiseless linear ramp has `ptp(deltas) == 0` — every segment
rises by exactly the same amount. So does a constant signal. The naive
"no spread → build a band around zero wide enough to swallow everything →
all-SAME" rule handles the constant case correctly and encodes **a
monotonic ramp as SAME**, which is the worst possible output of a trend
encoder.

They are now separated:

- `deltas_are_zero` (spread-free **and** at the origin) → `_all_same_cutlines`,
  a band at `±2·max|delta|` floored at the degeneracy tolerance, so every
  delta provably lands strictly inside. All-SAME.
- `deltas_are_flat` but not at the origin, **and** a learning mode is
  active → `_flat_nonzero_cutlines`, the interior points of an `(k+1)`-point
  grid across `[-|v|, +|v|]`, so the point mass lands in the extreme bin on
  its own side. A ramp reads as all-UP, a decay as all-DOWN.

The learning modes are short-circuited because Lloyd-Max cannot separate
identical samples and the empirical quantiles all coincide; `absolute` is
**not** short-circuited, because its cutlines came from a physical threshold
the caller stated and there is nothing to fall back from.

### 2.5 What `cutlines_degenerate` means

The brief ties this flag to two things: cutlines nudged apart, and a
constant input. I made it mean, precisely:

> these cutlines are not a real quantisation of this data

and set it in exactly three situations — a nudge happened; the deltas are
all zero (in **every** mode, because that is a statement about the data);
or a learning mode was handed a spread-free distribution and fell back on
the zero anchor for its bin widths.

It is deliberately **not** set for `absolute` mode on a noiseless ramp.
The deltas have no spread there, but the cutlines are the caller's stated
physical thresholds, un-nudged and entirely meaningful — flagging them
would be crying wolf, and `absolute` is exactly the mode a downstream
consumer would trust the flag on. `test_03c` pins this distinction in both
directions.

### 2.6 The two epsilons

| Constant | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `_DEGENERATE_REL` | `1e-9` | Multiplied by `max(max\|data\|, 1.0)` to get the "these deltas are constant" tolerance. Above the float64 round-off an OLS dot product accumulates over a few thousand samples (~1e-13 relative at worst), below any delta a real recording produces. Two decades of headroom either side. |
| `_ASCENT_EPS_REL` | `1e-9` | Multiplied by `max(max\|cutlines\|, std(deltas), 1.0)` to nudge collapsed cutlines apart. Same reasoning; the `max(..., 1.0)` floor keeps it representable when everything else is near zero. |

Both are relative, not absolute, because this repo's recordings are in
millivolts and a fixed absolute epsilon would be either meaningless or
catastrophic depending on the unit.

### 2.7 `min_same_halfwidth` semantics: a floor, not an override

Widening only (`c_lo = min(c_lo, -w)`, `c_hi = max(c_hi, +w)`), never
narrowing. If the learned SAME band is already wider than the noise floor,
the data is saying the quiescent range is genuinely wider than noise, and
narrowing it to the floor would discard that. Neighbouring cutlines are
pushed outward rather than the middle being clipped back, because the
caller's stated minimum has a justification behind it (a noise-floor
estimate) and the outer boundaries do not.

For even alphabet sizes it is a documented no-op — there is no SAME bin to
widen, only a cutline at zero — and `details["min_same_halfwidth_applied"]`
reports whether it did anything. That extra key is not in the brief's list;
the brief's list is explicitly a minimum ("at minimum") and a silently
ignored parameter is exactly the thing this codebase's house style objects
to elsewhere.

### 2.8 `same_fraction` is ignored when `alphabet_size > 3`

`quantile` mode has two rules. At k=3 the caller states what fraction
should be SAME and the cutlines deliver exactly that. At k>3 it places
equiprobable cutlines — classic SAX's own idea, applied to the empirical
delta distribution rather than to an assumed Gaussian. Forcing
`same_fraction` into the k>3 branch would make the parameter mean two
different things depending on another parameter's value, which is worse
than it having one clear scope. Documented in the function docstring.

### 2.9 `representatives` for the non-learned modes

The brief says "bin midpoints/medians otherwise". I used the **per-bin
median of the observed deltas**, falling back to the bin midpoint for an
empty interior bin and to `edge ± half a typical bin width` for an empty
outer bin. The median is what actually typifies a segment carrying that
symbol, and unlike a midpoint it is defined for the two unbounded outer
bins — which for a 3-symbol alphabet is two of the three.

### 2.10 `theil_sen` subsampling at 200 points

Kept at the brief's 200. `theilslopes` is O(sps²) in pairwise comparisons;
at sps=1000 that is 5·10⁵ pairs *per segment*, which turns a 3000-segment
encoding into minutes. The subsample uses `np.linspace(0, sps-1, 200)`, so
it keeps the first and last sample and the fitted span still covers the
full segment — which is what keeps the `× (sps - 1)` rescaling correct.
`details["theil_sen_subsampled"]` reports it, and `test_theil_sen_flags_its_own_subsampling`
asserts it fires at sps > 200.

### 2.11 `plot_trend_encoding(path=None)` by default

The brief's signature has `path` positional. I gave it a `None` default so
the adapter's `plot` hook — whose contract is "return a Figure", with the
caller deciding where it goes — can call it without inventing a temp path.
Passing a path still saves, and creates parent directories. Also added
`letter_threshold=120` as a keyword to match `plot_encoding_matplotlib`'s
signature shape rather than hard-coding the constant.

Two figure choices worth recording: the signal is drawn in **grey**, not
the usual signal blue, because the per-segment trend lines over it are
coloured by symbol and DOWN on a `coolwarm` scale is blue — a blue line
over a blue signal is invisible, which defeats the only purpose of that
panel. And panels 1, 2 and 4 have their x limits pinned to the same span
(`sharex` is unusable because panel 3's x axis is a delta axis, not time).

### 2.12 Dataset 6 uses `quantile`, not `learned`

The brief's table asks for a 5-symbol staircase producing exactly
`[0,1,2,3,4]` but does not say which threshold mode. `absolute` is a
3-symbol construction by definition. `learned` cannot be trusted to resolve
five clusters from five training points (k-means++ on five samples with
k=5 is a coin flip). `quantile` at k>3 places equiprobable cutlines and is
fully deterministic, and with exactly one segment per intended symbol
equiprobable *is* the correct answer. The test also asserts the deltas are
exactly `[-2,-1,0,1,2]`, so a passing symbol array cannot be a coincidence
of the cutline placement.

### 2.13 Test 8/9 dataset size raised from 300 to 3000 segments

**This is the one place I changed a test's setup after it failed, so it is
called out here and again in §3.** The brief specifies the assertion
`abs(UP_count - DOWN_count)/n < 0.1`; it does not specify `n`. At my first
choice of 300 segments the test failed at 0.113.

I investigated rather than loosening the bound, and the encoder is correct:
across 8 independent noise seeds `delta_mean` scattered symmetrically about
zero (−0.047 … +0.073, consistent with the predicted standard error of
0.031) with no bias. The problem was the test. Even with a perfectly
centred delta distribution, `UP − DOWN` is multinomial with
sd = `sqrt(n_seg · 0.5)`, so the imbalance statistic has
sd = `sqrt(0.5/n_seg)` — **0.041 at 300 segments, making a 0.1 bound a
2.4-sigma test that is flaky by construction.** At 3000 segments the sd is
0.013 and the same bound is a 7-sigma test. Measured max imbalance over 12
fresh seeds at the new size: **0.029**.

So the *expectation* was right and the *sample* was too small to support
it. I raised `n`, kept the assertion exactly as the brief states it, and
tightened the companion `|delta_mean|` bound from a meaningless 15-sigma to
a meaningful 5-sigma. The reasoning is recorded in a comment at the dataset
definition, not just here.

### 2.14 Miscellaneous

- **`ValueError` before any work.** Both enums, `alphabet_size`,
  `absolute_threshold` and `same_fraction` are validated at the top of
  `dsax()`, so a typo fails in microseconds rather than after a KDE on a
  160k-sample recording.
- **`data_nseg < 2` raises a real message.** `psax` reaches
  `data_len % data_nseg` with `data_nseg == 0` and dies with a bare
  `ZeroDivisionError`. dSAX needs ≥2 segments anyway, so it checks first
  and says what to change.
- **`_letter` is duplicated, not imported**, exactly as the brief requires
  and for the reason `_sax_common._letter` itself gives about `UI.plots`:
  `Working/` must not depend on `Adapters/`. The comment says so and names
  the other two copies.
- **Adapter sentinels.** `ParamSpec` has no nullable numeric type and a
  recipe hash must be JSON-serialisable, so `absolute_threshold` and
  `min_same_halfwidth` are exposed as floats with `0.0` meaning "unset".
  Safe for both: a zero absolute threshold puts both cutlines on the origin
  (which `dsax` rejects) and a zero half-width floor is a no-op, so neither
  has a meaningful zero value that the sentinel steals.
  `test_adapter_draft_zero_sentinels_mean_unset` pins it.
- **Tests print ASCII only**, because the pre-existing
  `test_analysis_modules` failure (see BASELINE.md) is a cp1252 console
  crash on a U+2500 box-drawing character and there was no reason to walk
  into the same wall.
- **The validation script imports its datasets from the test module**
  rather than redefining them, so the figures and the assertions provably
  describe the same arrays.

  *by construction*. The triangle's turning points sit exactly on segment
  boundaries in the unshifted encoding, so every segment is a pure ramp;
  shift by half a segment and every segment straddles a turning point, its
  net rise cancels, and the string goes from `UDUDUDUDUD` to all-SAME. A
  0.000 agreement is the encoder working correctly on an adversarial input,
  not a bug. **But it is the honest worst case for a periodic morphology
  whose period is commensurate with the segment length**, and it should
  make anyone cautious about reading a single dSAX string of an oscillatory
  signal as *the* answer.
- **6 (staircase) at 0.344** — same mechanism, five segments only, so each
  disagreement costs 0.2.
- **8 (white noise) at 0.391** — the floor you would expect: there is no
  structure at all for the encoding to be stable *about*. Random agreement
  for a 3-symbol string with these bin probabilities is ~0.37, so 0.391 is
  essentially "no reproducibility, because there is nothing to reproduce".
  This is the right answer for noise and is a useful negative control.
- **10, 11 at 0.615 / 0.500** — the realistic numbers. A smooth signal at a
  segment length comparable to its own timescale re-cuts meaningfully under
  a sub-segment shift.
- **7 (sharkfin) at 0.920** — the encouraging one, and the one that matters
  most for the actual use case: a *morphology* with a distinctive, aperiodic
  shape stays recognisable under re-cutting. The regex `UD{3,}` survives
  every offset tested.

**The practical consequence:** a single dSAX string of a fixed span is not
a stable fingerprint. Vocabulary matching should either be done at multiple
offsets and pooled, or restricted to patterns (like `UD{3,}`) that are
robust to one symbol changing. The 0.5 floor asserted in the script is a
catastrophic-bug guard, not a quality bar, and 0.712 clears it comfortably.

### 6.2 Estimator comparison — the design rationale, confirmed

Symbol-flip rate against the noiseless ground truth, 200 seeded repeats,
sps=50, threshold at 0.5 with per-segment rises sweeping through ±1.0 and
N(0, 0.2) noise:

| Estimator | flip rate | vs. `endpoints` |
|---|---:|---:|
| `endpoints` | 18.61 % | 1.00× |
| `robust_endpoints` (k=1) | 18.61 % | 1.00× |
| `ols_slope` | **6.58 %** | **0.35×** |
| `theil_sen` | **6.85 %** | **0.37×** |

**Interpretation:** the design rationale holds, and by a wide margin. An
`endpoints` delta has variance 2σ² no matter how long the segment is,
because it looks at two samples; an OLS delta has variance ≈ 12σ²/sps, so
at sps=50 the theoretical improvement is a factor of ~5 in variance, ~2.3
in standard deviation. The observed 2.8× reduction in flip rate is
consistent with that. **`ols_slope` is the correct default and the choice
is now measured, not asserted.**

`robust_endpoints` at k=1 is identical to `endpoints` to the last decimal,
which is the intended bit-identity (`test_12b`) showing up as an equality
in an independent measurement rather than only in a targeted assertion.
`theil_sen` buys nothing over `ols_slope` on Gaussian noise, at
considerably more compute — it would only pay off against outliers or
impulsive artefacts, which is exactly the case this synthetic signal does
not contain. Recommend `ols_slope` unless you are encoding a channel with
known spike artefacts.

### 6.3 Lloyd-Max vs. the noise floor — **the finding that matters**

White Gaussian noise, 120 000 samples, sps=40, 3000 segments. Both
half-widths in the normalised delta domain (the surrogate estimate is taken
on the z-normalised signal so the units match — comparing a raw-domain
half-width against a normalised cutline would be exactly the units error
the `delta_scale` discipline exists to prevent).

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---:|
| Learned (Lloyd-Max) SAME half-width | **0.336** |
| Surrogate noise-floor half-width (α = 0.95) | **1.041** |
| ratio surrogate / learned | **3.10×** |
| SAME fraction, learned cutlines | **46.5 %** (histogram 783 / 1395 / 822) |
| SAME fraction, noise floor injected | **94.6 %** (histogram 76 / 2838 / 86) |

**Interpretation — read this before using `learned` mode on real data.**

The signal here has **no trend anywhere**. It is pure noise. Lloyd-Max
nonetheless labels **53.5 % of its segments UP or DOWN**, in a near-perfect
783 / 1395 / 822 split, and the resulting encoding has an occupancy entropy
of 1.531 of a possible 1.585 bits — 96.6 % of ceiling. By every diagnostic
the shared `_sax_common` code offers, that encoding looks *excellent*. It
is entirely meaningless.

This is not a defect in Lloyd-Max; it is Lloyd-Max answering the question
it was asked. It minimises squared quantisation error against the observed
delta density, and the MSE-optimal way to represent a Gaussian with three
codewords is to split it into three roughly equal parts. "Is this rise
bigger than noise?" is a different question, and nothing in the pSAX
machinery asks it.

`surrogate_same_halfwidth` does ask it, and its answer is **3.1× wider**.
Impose it and the same noise encodes as 94.6 % SAME — which, for a signal
with no trend, is the correct answer (the residual 5.4 % is the α = 0.95
tail, by construction).

**What I would do with this, though the brief did not ask me to decide it:**
`min_same_halfwidth` should probably become the default rather than an
option, populated from a surrogate estimate computed once per channel. I
did **not** wire that in, because the brief is explicit that `dsax()` must
stay deterministic and that `surrogate_same_halfwidth` must not be called
from it, and because the correct α is a scientific choice about false-
positive rate that is yours to make, not mine. The hook, the estimator and
the measurement are all in place for you to make it.

Two caveats on the surrogate estimate, both real:

- The null is "no trend *beyond* the linear-Gaussian process with this
  magnitude spectrum". A signal whose slow drift *is* its spectrum will
  yield a large half-width, correctly reporting that segment-scale rises
  are unremarkable *for it*. That is a property of the null, not a bug, but
  it means the estimate is not transferable between channels.
- α = 0.95 is arbitrary. At α = 0.99 the band would be wider still.

## 7. Known limitations

1. **MINDIST is gone.** Classic SAX's headline theoretical property is that
   its symbolic distance lower-bounds the Euclidean distance between the
   original series, which is what makes SAX-indexed search *exact*. That
   proof rests on symbols encoding segment **means** under a known
   (Gaussian) distribution. dSAX encodes slopes under a learned
   distribution and has **no lower-bounding guarantee whatsoever**. Any
   dSAX-based similarity search is heuristic; it can miss true matches, and
   no pruning bound derived from it is safe. If you need exactness, dSAX
   must be a filter in front of a verification pass, never the final word.
   (1d-SAX — prior art [3] — retains a bound because it keeps the mean
   channel. That is the upgrade path if the guarantee turns out to matter.)

2. **Offset sensitivity.** Quantified in §6.1. Overall 0.712, but as low as
   0.117 for a periodic signal commensurate with the segment length. A
   single dSAX string of a fixed span is not a stable fingerprint.

3. **Drift dominance.** dSAX quantises a rise, so any monotonic trend makes
   every segment rise and the encoding collapses to all-UP carrying zero
   morphology (dataset 9: UP fraction 1.000, entropy 0.000). **Detrending
   is not optional on electrode data.** `test_09` asserts this failure mode
   *stays* reproducible, so that the encoder is never quietly changed to
   detrend on its own — which it must not, because then `normalize=False`
   would stop meaning what it says.

4. **Lloyd-Max is not a noise floor.** §6.3. On a trendless signal the
   default `learned` mode labels 53 % of segments as UP or DOWN and every
   entropy diagnostic reports it as an excellent encoding.

5. **High entropy does not mean signal.** Corollary of 4, and the reason
   §6.4 exists. Do not use occupancy entropy alone as an encoding-quality
   gate.

6. **`learned` mode consumes `np.random`** via `kmeanspp`, exactly as
   `psax()` does. Reproducibility requires seeding. `absolute` and
   `quantile` touch no RNG, which is why the exact-string tests use them.

7. **Even alphabet sizes have no SAME bin.** A consequence of zero-anchored
   folding, not a bug — but it means `min_same_halfwidth` is a documented
   no-op there, and a constant signal cannot be guaranteed all-one-symbol
   at even k (round-off deltas land either side of the cutline at zero).
   Use odd alphabet sizes for trend work.

8. **`theil_sen` above sps=200 fits a 200-point subsample.** Flagged in
   `details`, but it is an approximation, and on a segment with structure
   at the sub-subsample scale it is a different estimate from the one
   named.

9. **The surrogate null is per-channel.** §6.3's caveats — a half-width
   estimated on one recording does not transfer to another with different
   noise colour.

10. **Untested against real data.** Everything here is synthetic, per §0.6
    of the brief. No claim in this document has been checked against a
    fungal recording.

To wire dSAX into the UI when you want it:

   `git mv Working/Detection/sax/dsax_python/adapter_draft.py Adapters/detection_sax_dsax.py`

2. **Restore module-level registration.** In the moved file, change the
   import block and the `SPEC = AdapterSpec(...)` assignment:

   ```diff
   +from Adapters.base import AdapterSpec, AdapterResult, ParamSpec
   +from Adapters.registry import register
   ...
   -SPEC = AdapterSpec(
   +SPEC = register(AdapterSpec(
    ...
        derive=_derive,
   -)
   +))
   ```
   and delete `register_dsax_adapter()` (its docstring points here, so it
   has no purpose once promoted).

3. **Rewrite the module docstring.** The current one explains at length why
   the file is *not* under `Adapters/`. Replace it with the
   `detection_sax_psax.py`-style one-paragraph description.

4. **Update the two tests that assert it is not discoverable.**
   `tests/test_dsax.py::test_discover_adapters_does_not_register_dsax`
   must invert (assert `"detection.sax_dsax" in names`), and
   `test_adapter_draft_spec_is_well_formed` /
   `test_adapter_draft_run_and_derive_actually_work` /
   `test_adapter_draft_zero_sentinels_mean_unset` must import from
   `Adapters.detection_sax_dsax` instead. Also check
   `tests/test_sax_adapters.py` — it enumerates registered adapters and may
   assert on the count.

5. **THE UI WORK THAT IS ACTUALLY OUTSTANDING — the encoding view cannot
   render dSAX correctly as it stands.**

   The interactive encoding view (`UI.plots.build_encoding_panels`, driven
   from `UI/run_panel.py`) is the mirror of
   `Adapters._sax_common.plot_encoding_matplotlib`, and both do the same
   thing with `details`: they draw `cutlines_raw` as **horizontal amplitude
   bands** and overlay `paa_raw` as a step function, so that a symbol's
   band visually explains why that segment got that symbol.

   For dSAX that figure is not merely unhelpful, it is **physically
   meaningless**. `paa_raw` is a level, in millivolts. `cutlines_raw` is a
   threshold on a **rise**, in millivolts *per segment*. They are
   quantities of different dimension. Drawn on a shared y axis the bands
   will appear to explain symbols they had no part in choosing, and for a
   typical fungal channel (tens of mV standing potential, sub-mV
   excursions) the delta cutlines will collapse to a sliver near zero
   against the amplitude range, so the whole plot will look like every
   segment is in one band. It will look like a bug in dSAX. It is not.

   Concretely, what the shared view needs before dSAX can be shown in it —
   one of:

   - **(a) The minimal change.** Have the encoding view branch on a
     capability declared in `details` — e.g. a `cutline_domain` key with
     value `"amplitude"` (cSAX/pSAX) or `"delta"` (dSAX) — and for
     `"delta"` plot `deltas_raw` on the quantisation panel's y axis instead
     of `paa_raw`, with `cutlines_raw` as bands over *that*. dSAX already
     populates `deltas_raw`, `cutlines_raw`, `segment_starts` and
     `segment_ends`, so no encoder change is needed; **but `details` does
     not currently carry a `cutline_domain` key, and adding it means
     touching `csax.py` and `psax.py` to declare `"amplitude"`, which §0.3
     forbade me from doing.** That is the one upstream change promotion
     genuinely requires. (Alternatively the view can infer it from the
     presence of a `"deltas"` key, which needs no encoder change at all but
     is a duck-typing check rather than a declared contract.)

   - **(b) The better change.** Give the encoding view a second, optional
     panel row driven by whichever of `paa_raw` / `deltas_raw` is present,
     so a future TVA-style value+trend encoder can show both at once.
     dSAX already populates `paa`/`paa_raw` precisely so this costs nothing
     later (§3.6 of the brief, and it is why those keys exist in a trend
     encoder at all).

   Until one of those lands, the promoted adapter's `plot` hook still works
   — it points at `plot_trend_encoding`, which is correct and
   self-contained — so **static figures and SLURM output are fine today;
   only the interactive in-panel view is blocked.**

6. **Decide the `min_same_halfwidth` default** before exposing `learned`
   mode to users, in the light of §6.3. The current adapter default of
   `0.0` (unset) reproduces the brief exactly and gives the 46.5 %-SAME
   behaviour on noise.

7. **Re-run** `python "Experimentation/Detection experiments/run_dsax_validation.py"`
   and both test files after any of the above.
